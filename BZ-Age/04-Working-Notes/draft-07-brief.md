## The cast: one household that becomes five strangers

Part 1 opens in one house in 5000 BCE. Six people belong to the illustrative household; five are present while the trading brother is away. Their work overlaps. Each stands for a line the series follows. The lines are a storytelling device and the prologue says so once. In each part a documented person "wears" a line where the archives allow it.

| Line | Starts as | Becomes |
|---|---|---|
| **The Farmer** (and his sister) | forager, then field hand | temple tenant, conquered villager, debtor, conscript, refugee, free hill farmer. The one who never gets a new job and feeds the rest. His sister is the one the ledger takes: grinder, weaver, captive. |
| **The Keeper** | the grandmother who minds the grain store | headman, temple steward, king, god-king, Great King, last king, village chief, a hero in a poem |
| **The Counter** | the boy who tallies sheep with pebbles | token-keeper, scribe, poet, letter-writer, palace clerk, the grandson who can't read, the man with 22 letters |
| **The Carrier** | the brother who walks for obsidian | barge-puller, donkey merchant, ship merchant, (some) raider, Phoenician |
| **The Maker** | the cousin who is good with stone | arsenic-copper smith, bronze caster, chariot builder, palace smith on rations, scrap melter, ironsmith |

**Why this cast and not fixed roles.** The cast makes changes in work and dependence intelligible. It does not prove a universal progression or a single cause of collapse. Some work stays local; some crosses regions. The aftermath contains continuing trade, states, and specialists as well as smaller communities.

**Dosage.** A check-in of under a hundred words at the top of each part ("where is the family now"), then one or two lines carry that part. Not every line appears in every part.

## Format rules

1. **One spine per part, in time order.** One explicit flashback allowed per part, announced as such.
2. **The top-of-part card.** When, how long since last time, where on the map, where the family is. Same shape every part.
3. **Simple questions drive the hour.** Three or four per part, asked plainly, never labelled as "a child's question".
4. **Start from what the listener owns** (the clock, the mailbox, a mortgage, Noah, Goliath, the Minotaur) and walk back to the origin.
5. **Name by role first.** A name is given only if the person returns or the name is the point. Recurring powers carry a fixed tag every time (Assur the merchant city; Assyria the northern Iraqi kingdom; Mitanni the horse kingdom; Alashiya on Cyprus).
6. **Side quests are welcome** when they are marked on the way out and on the way back, and pay off to the listener or to later history.
7. **Say it once.** No idea is argued twice. Callbacks get one cue.

## Voice

- The narrator has opinions and states them: "I think", "my money is on", "I don't buy that". The caveat comes once, after the position.
- Uncertainty is stated once per topic, plainly, and then the story moves.
- Plain words. Contractions. American units (miles, feet, pounds).
- Lines that claim the narrator's own experience are kept to a minimum and listed in each file's revision notes for the writer to confirm or cut.

## Prose rules (so it does not sound generated)

Banned or rationed to near zero: the "it's not X, it's Y" reveal; stacked groups of three; the one-line paragraph after every beat; "genuinely", "honestly", "sit with that", "here's the thing", "worth pausing on", "let that sink in"; questions asked only so the next sentence can answer them (the part's driving questions excepted); em-dash asides; telling the listener a thing is remarkable.

## Writing for ElevenLabs

- No markdown emphasis, brackets, symbols or parentheses inside narration.
- No sentence with more than one aside. Long sentences are fine when they run forward.
- Hard names are few; pronunciations live in `BZ-Age-draft07-pronunciation.md`.
- Section breaks are real pauses.

## Visuals

Each draft file carries sparse cue lines on their own, in the form `[VISUAL: …]`. They are stripped from the narration assembly and collected into `BZ-Age-draft07-visual-cues.md`. Four kinds only: the top-of-part card, a map, an object, a simple diagram. Around eight to twelve per part.

## Length

v0.7.2 as built is about 40,500 words (about 4.5 hours at 150 words a minute), down from 66,000. The writer's ruling (Nov 16): whatever length serves the story. v0.7.2 adds scenes and marked asides where parts were thin; no word target is set.

## Verification

New facts and all paraphrased ancient speech are listed in each file's revision notes with a confidence mark. Direct quotation is used only where draft-06 already carried a verified quote, or where the wording is standard. Everything else is given as paraphrase and says so.

## Addendum — rules settled in the review exchange with ChatGPT (2026-11-16)

Source: the Notion page "BZ-Age — Parts 1–6 editorial review & Claude critique" (Round 1, ChatGPT) and its child page "Round 2 — Claude's response". Per-issue dispositions are in each part file's revision notes.

1. **No prior knowledge assumed.** Every people, city, kingdom and institution is introduced on first use by what it is, where it is, and why it matters now, in one or two sentences. It is reintroduced by role after a long gap.
2. **Orientation is spoken.** Date cards are for viewers. Every part's first minute says when, how far from the last part, and where, with one familiar anchor.
3. **The family is five kinds of work, not a bloodline.** Six people belong to the first household, with the brother away at the opening. Later parts say "the people doing the Carrier's kind of work", or name a documented person. Real viewpoints (Enheduanna, the Pylos desk) are retired or dated out loud.
4. **Attribute in the sentence; doubt once.** "Xenophon says", "a later legend", "the leading explanation". One caveat where the evidence changes category. Evidence bookkeeping stays in the revision notes.
5. **The writer's ruling on caution (Nov 16), which settles the difference between the two reviewers:** build the story on what is solid and agreed, and state it plainly. Use caution only where the evidence is thin, and be upfront about it there. Leave room for occasional asides that are openly exploratory: a what-if, a thought experiment, an imagined person, each announced as such. Solid claims do not carry "I think".
6. **Explanations are scoped, not deleted.** "The first big cities of the ancient world", "one answer, not a law of nature".
7. **One analogy per passage**, and it must explain how something worked.
8. **One forward warning per part at most.** No end-of-part refrain that everyone depends on everyone.
9. **Imagined scenes are marked** ("picture", "I'm inventing the man, but not his problem") and carry no invented statistics.
10. **Paraphrase is called paraphrase** ("in effect"), without performed forgetfulness. All quotations go to a quote ledger before recording (queued).
